# Signal Exchange: recorded product demonstration

## Overview

Use the deployed application's midnight, brass and cyan identity. The product, not decorative animation, is the hero. Show real browser recordings with clearly labelled excerpts from actual WebMCP results; never imitate a Codex chat window. Human-facing controls and agent-facing contracts remain visually distinct.

## Typography

- Georgia 700: chapter headings and human decisions, 64px.
- Arial 400/700: narration-supporting explanations, 28px. Preserve the prior video's interface fallback; browser footage retains the real site's own rendering.
- Courier New 700: tool names and exact response fields, 22–26px.
- Tabular numbers for prices and balances. No remote font dependencies.

## Elevation

Thin brass rules and solid midnight panels. A 1px frame distinguishes unmodified browser footage from editorial annotations. No perspective distortion of text and no visual treatment that obscures evidence.

## Components

- Large browser recording: actual screen pixels, cropped only when needed for legibility.
- Recorded WebMCP trace: input/response excerpts, explicitly not a chat reconstruction.
- Chapter strip: human controls, agent tools, approval, delivery and implementation.
- Source footer: live demo; synthetic samples and simulated credits; AI narration.
- Exact terms callout: 20 bid, 22 counteroffer, 100 to 78 wallet.
- Brand compass: captured SVG in opener and closer.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Do keep the page and the results legible at 1080p.
- Do use restrained 0.35s crossfades between related chapters.
- Do pause on the actual participant's approval and the returned rows.
- Do identify shortened waits and quoted tool excerpts honestly.
- Don't invent tool output, fake typing, fake mouse clicks or fake consent.
- Don't confuse the manual supplier demonstration operated by Codex with the participant's real approval click.
- Don't claim live payments, verified sellers, secure DRM or a multi-user service.
- Don't add music, visual noise or gratuitous motion over the instructional recording.
